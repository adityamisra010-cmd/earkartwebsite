# Phase C — Customer Experience Blueprint

**Experience first, pages second.** This maps the complete patient journey as *felt experience* across all channels (web, phone, WhatsApp, clinic, home visit). It deliberately ignores layout. Each of the 16 stages carries: Customer Goals · Customer Questions · Customer Emotions · Business Objectives · Digital Touchpoints · Offline Touchpoints · Opportunities · Failure Risks · Success Metrics.

**Spine alignment:** these 16 stages expand the v1 journey (Unaware → … → Advocating). The peak-end rule governs the whole arc (Rule A-105): the brand is remembered at its emotional peak and its end, not its average.

---

## Stage 1 — Discovery
**Goals:** Stumble onto something relevant while searching a symptom, reading, or being sent a link by family.
**Questions:** "Is what I'm experiencing normal?" "Who are these people?" "Is this trustworthy or just selling?"
**Emotions:** Mild worry, curiosity, guardedness, sometimes denial.
**Business objectives:** Organic reach; first-impression trust within 2–30s; capture into a low-commitment next step.
**Digital:** SEO blog/Hub article, paid social, search result, shared WhatsApp link, Google Business profile.
**Offline:** Word of mouth, ENT/GP mention, clinic signage, family conversation.
**Opportunities:** Lead with the user's lived moment (A-003); fast trust signal calibrated to entry (A-008); offer the free check as the soft entry.
**Failure risks:** Reads as an ad → instant bounce; slow page; stigma-triggering headline; no obvious "for someone like me."
**Metrics:** Organic sessions, bounce rate, scroll depth, entry→micro-conversion rate.

## Stage 2 — Education
**Goals:** Understand what's happening to me/my parent without being scared or sold to.
**Questions:** "What causes this?" "How serious is it?" "Hearing vs. understanding — why can I hear but not catch words?"
**Emotions:** Relief at clarity, occasional anxiety, growing engagement.
**Business objectives:** Establish authority; correct false mental models; move user from Aware → Curious.
**Digital:** Hub pillar/cluster articles, explainer illustrations, FAQs, short video.
**Offline:** Leaflets, ENT explanation, family discussion.
**Opportunities:** Progressive disclosure (A-025); story-based myth correction (A-063); stage-tagged content with next-stage links (A-101); the "most useful thing" standard (A-100).
**Failure risks:** Too clinical/academic; fear-led framing; reading level mismatch; dead-end pages with no scent forward.
**Metrics:** Time on page (>3–5 min), pages per session, blog→tool/booking rate.

## Stage 3 — Trust Building
**Goals:** Decide whether earKART specifically is credible and safe.
**Questions:** "Are these real clinicians?" "Will they pressure me?" "Do people like me trust them?"
**Emotions:** Cautious evaluation, hope, fear of being upsold.
**Business objectives:** Move Curious → Considering; earn the right to ask for an action.
**Digital:** Audiologist profiles, patient stories, reviews, trust numbers, "no pressure" promise, clinic photos.
**Offline:** Referral from a trusted doctor/friend, reputation in the community.
**Opportunities:** Lead with relationship trust and real humans (A-010, A-057); address the specific trust dimension per page (A-009); honest limitations (A-013).
**Failure risks:** Stock photos exposed; unsourced claims; awards with no name; review section ignored or full of unanswered complaints.
**Metrics:** Return visits, newsletter signups, story/profile engagement, review sentiment.

## Stage 4 — Assessment (self-screening)
**Goals:** Get a private, no-stakes read on my hearing; bring data to a family conversation (Priya) or self-check discreetly (Arjun).
**Questions:** "How bad is it?" "Will this judge me?" "What do I do with the result?"
**Emotions:** Nervous anticipation → relief or sober concern.
**Business objectives:** Primary micro-conversion; qualify and orient leads; create a confident next step.
**Digital:** Online Hearing Check tool, results screen, optional emailed PDF, follow-up sequence.
**Offline:** Family member guiding an elder through it on a phone.
**Opportunities:** Show results before any email gate (A-097); honest "screening not diagnosis" (A-093); calm, non-alarming result tones; progress bar (A-113); non-audio path (A-079).
**Failure risks:** False-positive audio test causing alarm; gating results behind email; alarming language; tool too complex for Rajan.
**Metrics:** Tool start→completion rate, post-result email opt-in, result→booking rate.

## Stage 5 — Appointment (booking)
**Goals:** Book a test easily, at a convenient clinic/time, with confidence it's reversible.
**Questions:** "Will it hurt?" "How long?" "Will I be pressured to buy?" "How much?" "Can I cancel?"
**Emotions:** Resolve mixed with anxiety; fear of commitment.
**Business objectives:** Macro-conversion; minimize no-shows; capture clean data for clinic prep.
**Digital:** Booking flow, clinic finder, confirmation page + email/WhatsApp, calendar invite.
**Offline:** Phone booking, family booking on patient's behalf.
**Opportunities:** Pre-fill from context (A-043); one decision per step (A-027); confidence checkpoint + reversibility (A-044, A-046); justify the decision immediately after (A-045); design the web→phone→clinic transition (A-106).
**Failure risks:** Too many fields; hidden price; no mobile persistent CTA; unclear what happens next; data not reaching the CRM/clinic.
**Metrics:** Booking start→complete rate, mobile parity, no-show rate, data-integrity rate.

## Stage 6 — Clinic Visit
**Goals:** Arrive without dread; feel welcomed and unhurried; understand each step.
**Questions:** "Where do I go?" "Who will I meet?" "What happens first?"
**Emotions:** Anticipatory anxiety → reassurance (if pre-framed) → comfort.
**Business objectives:** Deliver on every web promise (peak-end); high satisfaction; set up the diagnosis conversation.
**Digital:** Pre-visit "what to expect" page with waiting-room/clinic/audiologist photos; directions; reminder.
**Offline:** Reception warmth, named audiologist, the physical room, pace of care.
**Opportunities:** Anticipatory-anxiety reduction via pre-visit content; operational standards matching web promises (A-105); the "no pressure" promise honored in the room.
**Failure risks:** Reality contradicts the website (rushed, salesy, cold) → trust collapse; long wait; audiologist differs from the named profile.
**Metrics:** No-show rate, in-clinic satisfaction (CSAT), promise-match score.

## Stage 7 — Diagnosis
**Goals:** Understand my audiogram and what it means for my life, in plain language.
**Questions:** "What's actually wrong?" "Is it bad?" "What are my options — honestly?"
**Emotions:** Vulnerability, relief at clarity, possible grief, hope.
**Business objectives:** Build decision confidence; transition to appropriate recommendation without pressure.
**Digital:** Audiogram explainer, emailed results summary, "questions to ask" resource.
**Offline:** Audiologist walking through results in plain language, no rush.
**Opportunities:** What→why→how sequencing (A-031); absolute-risk framing (A-034); empower patient questions (A-095); reflect dignity and hope (A-005, A-006).
**Failure risks:** Jargon dump; alarmist delivery; pivoting straight to a sales pitch; numbers without meaning.
**Metrics:** Comprehension/CSAT, proceed-to-recommendation rate, complaint rate.

## Stage 8 — Device Recommendation
**Goals:** Find the right device for *my* life and budget without being oversold.
**Questions:** "Which one is right for me?" "Why this one?" "Am I being upsold?" "How visible is it?"
**Emotions:** Overwhelm risk, hope, suspicion of upselling (Meera), appearance anxiety.
**Business objectives:** Appropriate selection; trial initiation; protect trust while optimizing mix.
**Digital:** Solutions organized by lifestyle (A-037), ≤3 compared (A-024), recommendation with caveat (A-036), honest pricing with daily framing (A-039), trial offer.
**Offline:** Audiologist's personalized recommendation and demonstration.
**Opportunities:** Anchor then contextualize (A-038); concretize future benefit before price (A-040); endowment-framed trial (A-041); separate info from recommendation (A-096).
**Failure risks:** Catalog overload; appearance/stigma not addressed; price shock; perceived upselling; genuine decoy crossing into manipulation.
**Metrics:** Trial initiation rate, recommendation acceptance, perceived-pressure score.

## Stage 9 — Purchase
**Goals:** Commit with confidence; understand cost, financing, warranty, and what's included.
**Questions:** "Is this the right call?" "What if it doesn't work for me?" "What does the price actually cover?"
**Emotions:** Pain of paying, hope, need for reassurance.
**Business objectives:** Conversion; low buyer's remorse; clean handoff to onboarding.
**Digital:** Transparent pricing/financing, confirmation, warranty/returns clearly stated.
**Offline:** In-clinic payment, fitting scheduling, human reassurance.
**Opportunities:** Reframe lump sum as daily cost (A-039); justify the decision post-purchase (A-045); make reversibility/return clear (A-046); financing over fake offers (A-058); never shame budget choices (A-084).
**Failure risks:** Hidden fees; pain-of-paying spikes abandonment; unclear return policy; remorse → cancellation/return.
**Metrics:** Close rate, return rate, financing uptake, post-purchase regret signals.

## Stage 10 — Onboarding (fitting & first weeks)
**Goals:** Adjust to the device; learn to use and care for it; know help is there.
**Questions:** "Why does my own voice sound odd?" "Is this normal?" "How do I clean it / change batteries?"
**Emotions:** Excitement, frustration at adjustment, doubt ("did I waste money?").
**Business objectives:** Prevent the drawer-abandonment failure; set up habits and adherence.
**Digital:** Onboarding email/WhatsApp series, video tutorials, portal setup, troubleshooting.
**Offline:** Fitting appointment, follow-up adjustment visits.
**Opportunities:** Habit loops for daily wear/cleaning (A-048); humane reminder cadence (A-049); normalize the adjustment period honestly (A-013); progress/streaks where motivating (A-112).
**Failure risks:** Adjustment frustration unaddressed → device abandoned; no proactive check-in; tutorials too complex.
**Metrics:** Adherence/daily-wear rate, follow-up attendance, early-return rate, support-resolution time.

## Stage 11 — Aftercare
**Goals:** Get help quickly when something's wrong; feel cared for, not forgotten.
**Questions:** "It's not working — what now?" "Who do I call?" "Is this covered?"
**Emotions:** Frustration, anxiety, gratitude when resolved fast.
**Business objectives:** Retention; trust repair; turn problems into loyalty.
**Digital:** Portal support, troubleshooting, repair request, WhatsApp support, FAQ.
**Offline:** Clinic repairs, replacement, in-person support.
**Opportunities:** Service-recovery path designed (A-108); sympathetic specific support tone (A-073); one consistent voice (A-098); honest bot-to-human handoff (A-099).
**Failure risks:** Slow/opaque support; bounced between channels; patient feels abandoned after the sale.
**Metrics:** First-contact resolution, support CSAT, churn after first issue.

## Stage 12 — Maintenance
**Goals:** Keep the device working with minimal effort; restock supplies easily.
**Questions:** "When do I clean/replace what?" "How do I reorder batteries/domes?"
**Emotions:** Low-grade routine; mild annoyance if friction appears.
**Business objectives:** Sustained adherence; recurring revenue (consumables); habit reinforcement.
**Digital:** Maintenance reminders, reorder flow, care content, portal maintenance log.
**Offline:** Clinic cleaning service, in-person resupply.
**Opportunities:** Habit cadence and tracking (A-048, A-049); reorder pre-filled and one-tap (A-043); value-reminder messaging (A-051).
**Failure risks:** Reminders feel like nagging → opt-out; reorder friction → device degrades and gets abandoned.
**Metrics:** Reorder rate, maintenance-reminder engagement, sustained adherence.

## Stage 13 — Annual Reviews
**Goals:** Recheck hearing; re-tune or upgrade as needs change.
**Questions:** "Has my hearing changed?" "Do I need a new device?" "Is it worth re-testing?"
**Emotions:** Mild reluctance, reassurance once booked.
**Business objectives:** Re-engagement; upgrade pipeline; long-term LTV.
**Digital:** Annual reminder, easy re-book, audiogram-on-file comparison.
**Offline:** Review appointment, re-fitting.
**Opportunities:** Relationship calendar (A-050); normalize routine review (A-047); frame as clarity not problem-hunting (A-056).
**Failure risks:** No proactive reminder → silent lapse; re-booking friction; review feels like an upsell trap.
**Metrics:** Annual-review attendance, upgrade rate, reactivation rate.

## Stage 14 — Referrals
**Goals:** Tell family/friends/doctor about a good experience — without it feeling awkward.
**Questions:** "Is this worth recommending?" "How do I share it?" "What's in it for them (not me)?"
**Emotions:** Pride, goodwill, slight social hesitation.
**Business objectives:** Lower CAC; trust-transfer growth; activate the strongest channel.
**Digital:** Referral flow, shareable branded guides, "gift a hearing check," review request.
**Offline:** Personal recommendation, doctor referral.
**Opportunities:** Ask at peak satisfaction (A-052); give social currency not a sales ask (A-053); enable doctor/family referral paths (A-054).
**Failure risks:** Ask mistimed (during friction); referral feels transactional/cheap; sharing is high-effort.
**Metrics:** Referral participation, referred bookings, referral CAC.

## Stage 15 — Loyalty
**Goals:** Feel like a valued, known patient; belong; trust earKART for the long term.
**Questions:** "Do they remember me?" "Am I getting ongoing value?"
**Emotions:** Belonging, trust, identity alignment ("I take care of my hearing").
**Business objectives:** Retention; advocacy; multi-year LTV.
**Digital:** Portal continuity, value-reminder comms, community features (with safeguards), recognition.
**Offline:** Continuity of audiologist relationship, recognition at the clinic.
**Opportunities:** Relationship progression model new→regular→advocate (A-050); reflect value back (A-051); community belonging (loyalty psychology).
**Failure risks:** Patient feels like a transaction once warranty ends; no recognition; generic blasts replace relationship.
**Metrics:** 3-year retention, NPS, advocacy rate, portal engagement.

## Stage 16 — Re-engagement
**Goals:** Be gently brought back after lapsing (missed reviews, dormant device, old technology).
**Questions:** "Is it time to come back?" "Has anything improved since my last device?"
**Emotions:** Mild guilt risk (must be avoided), curiosity, renewed motivation.
**Business objectives:** Reactivate dormant patients; recover at-risk LTV.
**Digital:** Win-back sequences, "what's new since your last visit," personalized re-book.
**Offline:** Outreach call, community event invite.
**Opportunities:** Never guilt the lapsed user (Forbidden Pattern 1); frame around new possibility and clarity (A-056); personalize on stage/history within privacy law (A-111, A-097).
**Failure risks:** Guilt-laden or nagging tone → unsubscribe; irrelevant blasts; privacy overreach.
**Metrics:** Reactivation rate, win-back conversion, unsubscribe rate.

## Cross-Stage Principles

- **Peak-end governs the whole arc.** Invest disproportionately in the emotional peak (Diagnosis/Onboarding success) and every *end* (each confirmation, each support resolution). A great site with a poor phone line averages to a poor brand (A-105).
- **Every transition is designed, not assumed** (A-106). The seams — web→phone, phone→clinic, purchase→onboarding, issue→resolution — are where trust leaks.
- **One voice across all touchpoints** (A-098). The person who reassures on the homepage is the same person on WhatsApp, on the phone, and in the room.
- **The family is a participant at every stage**, not a bystander (A-055). Design for the committee.
- **Emotion is the through-line.** The brand's job across all 16 stages is to keep net anxiety falling and net dignity rising.
