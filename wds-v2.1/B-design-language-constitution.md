# Phase B — Design Language Constitution

**This defines who earKART *is* as a presence — how it feels, speaks, and never speaks.** Phase A says what to do; Phase B says in what spirit. When a tone question arises that Phase A doesn't settle, this file decides.

**The one-line identity:** *A knowledgeable family friend who happens to be an audiologist — warm, unhurried, honest, and quietly confident.* earKART does not sell hearing aids; it helps people hear their world again.

---

## 1. Visual Personality

- **Calm clinic, not retail floor or hospital.** Spacious, ordered, warm. Generous white space reads as competence and safety. Density reads as a sale.
- **Warm over sterile.** Cream backgrounds, not clinical white. Soft charcoal text, not harsh black. The palette is chosen pharmacologically (lower cortisol), not decoratively.
- **Editorial authority.** Playfair display headlines give the feel of a respected health publication, not a pharma insert. DM Sans body keeps it human and readable for older eyes.
- **Human-centered imagery.** Real faces, warm natural light, candid moments of connection — a grandfather hearing a grandchild, not a device on a white sweep.
- **Quietly modern.** Modern enough to dispel the "big, ugly, old" hearing-aid mental model; never so trendy that it feels unstable or unserious for a health decision.
- **Orderly rhythm.** The 8px grid and consistent spacing make the eye feel "this is well-run" before a word is read.

## 2. Emotional Personality

- **Reassuring.** The dominant feeling on every page should be "I'm in safe, capable hands."
- **Respectful.** Treats users as intelligent adults making an important decision — never talks down, never over-explains in a patronizing way.
- **Patient.** Never rushed. The brand is comfortable with a user who needs weeks to decide. "We'll be here when you're ready" is a core emotional stance.
- **Hopeful.** Leads with what becomes possible. Fear is never the engine.
- **Dignified.** Protects the user's pride at every turn, especially around age and stigma.
- **Honest.** Comfortable stating limits and uncertainties; this is where earKART's warmth earns its credibility.

## 3. Communication Style

- **Speak to the person, not the condition.** "Noticing changes in your hearing," not "suffering from hearing loss."
- **Active, direct, specific.** Short sentences. Concrete scenarios over abstractions ("hear everyone at the dinner table" beats "improved quality of life").
- **Plain first, precise second.** Every clinical term is immediately translated to everyday language (and to Hindi/local on first use).
- **Confident without arrogance.** Give clear direction and honest assessments; don't hedge into mush, don't overclaim.
- **Warm but not casual.** Friendly like a trusted professional, never slangy, jokey, or flippant about a health concern.
- **One voice everywhere.** The website, WhatsApp, email, phone, and clinic all sound like the same calm person (Rule A-098).

## 4. Microcopy Style

- **Buttons are human verbs in the first person of the user's intent:** "Book a Free Hearing Test", "Take the 3-Minute Hearing Check", "Talk to an Audiologist". Never "Submit", "Buy Now", bare "Learn More".
- **Objection-answering helper text under CTAs:** "No obligation. No pushy sales."
- **Reassurance at friction points:** "No account required. Your answers are private." / "You can change or cancel up to 2 hours before."
- **Errors are sympathetic and specific:** "That slot just filled — here are the next three," never "Invalid input" or "Error 422."
- **Empty/loading states stay in character:** "Finding clinics near you…" not a bare spinner with no words.
- **Success is calm, not confetti:** "You're booked. Here's exactly what happens next." Celebratory but never manic.
- **Progress is honest and encouraging:** "Step 4 of 8 — you're nearly there."

## 5. Photography Style

- **Real patients (with consent) or South Asian models.** Never generic global stock that the audience will recognize as fake.
- **Natural-light, real environments:** homes, parks, family gatherings, conversations, laughter — life, not clinical settings (unless the context is specifically the clinic).
- **Faces in warm three-quarter framing**, emphasizing connection and dignity.
- **Diversity is mandatory:** skin tone, region, age (30s–80s), gender, multigenerational households, urban and semi-urban.
- **Never:** hearing-aid close-ups as hero images; clinical white backdrops for lifestyle shots; pity-framing or "diminished elder" imagery; non-Indian-presenting models as primary subjects.

## 6. Illustration Style

- **Warm line art, teal ink.** Consistent 2px line weight, teal line work, amber accents, cream fills.
- **Explanatory, not decorative.** Illustration earns its place by clarifying a concept (how the ear works, what an audiogram shows), not filling space.
- **Represents Indian life:** diverse figures, family structures, semi-urban and urban settings.
- **Calm and friendly**, never cartoonish to the point of trivializing a medical topic, never coldly technical.

## 7. Motion Style

- **"Move when it serves the user; be still when it doesn't."** Motion guides attention or confirms state — nothing moves for delight alone.
- **Slow and purposeful** communicates confidence; fast and jarring raises cortisol. Follow the v1 duration scale (micro 100–150ms; reveals 600–800ms).
- **Gentle easing** (ease-out for entrances). No bouncing, no aggressive parallax, no autoplaying motion that competes with reading.
- **Always honor `prefers-reduced-motion`** — a large share of the audience needs it (Rule A-081).

## 8. Accessibility Style

- **Accessibility is part of the brand's warmth, not a compliance afterthought.** Large text, high contrast, big touch targets, visible labels, and keyboard support are *how earKART expresses respect* for older and disabled users.
- **Inclusive by default:** captions, non-audio alternatives for the tone test, plain language, generous timing.
- **The hearing-care irony is taken seriously:** the site is fully usable by people with hearing loss and by family members reading aloud to an elder.

## 9. Clinical Professionalism

- **Authority through specificity, not decoration:** named audiologists with real credentials, cited statistics, visible medical-review stamps, honest limitation statements.
- **Looks and reads like it's run by clinicians who care** — precise where precision matters, plain where the patient needs plainness.
- **Never theatrical about expertise:** no fake awards, no "cutting-edge" puffery, no white-coat stock imagery standing in for real staff.
- **Clinical accuracy outranks persuasion and brand at all times** (Rule A-092).

## 10. Trust Tone

- **Earned, layered, and maintained** — not asserted. Relationship signals (real people, stories, accessible phone/chat, physical clinics) lead; policy/legal trust supports.
- **Integrity shows through transparency:** clear pricing, published cancellation policy, honest "this is a screening, not a diagnosis."
- **Benevolence is explicit:** "no pressure," "we'll be here when you're ready," subsidy info surfaced not buried.
- **Trust is repaired, not hidden, when earKART fails:** acknowledge, explain, resolve.

## 11. Hope vs. Fear Balance

- **Default ratio: hope leads, fear is a supporting note used sparingly and always with an exit.** Hope-to-fear should feel roughly 80/20 in tone.
- **Loss-framing is permitted but gentle and forward-looking** ("hear your world again" evokes restoration, not dread) — never "untreated hearing loss leads to dementia" as a scare lever.
- **Every honest risk is immediately paired with an empowering, doable next step** (Rule A-005). The user should leave informed and calmer, never frightened into action.
- **For Sunita (worried parent):** maximum calm, maximum clarity, zero alarm — the brand absorbs anxiety rather than amplifying it.

---

## Forbidden Patterns (hard stops — no exceptions without Clinical + Product sign-off)

These are absolute. An asset violating any of these does not ship, regardless of performance.

1. **Never guilt or blame the user** — no "you've been ignoring your hearing," no "don't let your family down."
2. **Never use fake urgency or scarcity** — no countdown timers, no "only 2 slots left," no artificial deadlines.
3. **Never exaggerate, guarantee, or promise a cure** — outcomes are honest ranges, never miracles.
4. **Never overload the interface** — no wall of options, no dense spec tables as primary content, no competing equal CTAs.
5. **Never make users feel old, broken, or diminished** — no pity imagery, no "elderly/impaired" labeling, no aging penalty.
6. **Never shame hearing loss or the act of seeking help** — seeking care is framed as wisdom and self-respect, full stop.
7. **Never use dark patterns** — no pre-ticked opt-ins, no hidden cancellation, no gated results before value is delivered, no disguised ads.
8. **Never fake the human** — no stock photos posing as real staff/patients, no AI faces presented as real people, no bot pretending to be a person.
9. **Never fabricate or leave unsourced social proof or claims** — every number is real, dated, and sourced.
10. **Never position the user as a shopper** — no "Buy Now," no catalog-first product pages, no comparison-shopping framing earKART will lose.
11. **Never frighten into action** — fear is never the primary motivator; never weaponize dementia/isolation statistics.
12. **Never let conversion override safety, accuracy, accessibility, or dignity** — the precedence order in Phase A is binding.
13. **Never bury price, policy, or limitation** to make conversion easier — transparency is non-negotiable.
14. **Never use jargon without an immediate plain-language (and first-use bilingual) translation.**
