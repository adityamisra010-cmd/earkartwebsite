# Phase G — Experience Narrative

**The benchmark.** These are the felt journeys the design must produce. When a future decision is unclear, ask: *does this move the persona through their emotional arc the way the narrative describes?* If not, it's wrong. Each narrative tracks **every emotional transition** — that arc is the acceptance test.

Personas use the canonical v1 names: **Rajan, Priya, Arjun, Sunita, Meera, Rohan**, plus internal stakeholders.

---

## Narrative 1 — Rajan, 68 (The Reluctant Elder) — *the flagship journey*

**Denial → mild defensiveness → being seen → curiosity → cautious hope → dignity-preserving decision → relief → belonging.**

Rajan's daughter sends him a WhatsApp link. He almost doesn't open it — he's tired of being treated like he's "losing it." He opens it grudgingly. **(Defensiveness.)** The first thing he reads isn't "you have hearing loss." It's: *Asking people to repeat themselves. Turning the TV up a little louder. Nodding along when you're not quite sure what was said.* He stops. That's exactly his Tuesday. **(Being seen — the first transition: from defensive to recognized.)** The page doesn't call him old. It says these moments are common and "don't make you less." **(Defensiveness drains; dignity intact.)**

He scrolls and sees a man his age, in a sunlit home, laughing with a grandchild — not a device, not a clinic. **(Curiosity. The "big ugly hearing aid" image in his head is quietly contradicted.)** He reads Krishnamurthy's story — a retired bank officer from Coimbatore who waited too long and wishes he hadn't. **(Identification. "Someone like me did this.")**

His daughter offers to take the 3-minute check *with* him. No account, answers private. The questions are large, simple, kind. The result is amber: "it may be worth talking to an audiologist" — not "you're deaf," not alarm. **(Cautious concern, not fear.)** It says the decision is his and there's no rush. **(Autonomy preserved — the critical transition for Rajan: he doesn't feel pushed.)**

He thinks for two weeks. earKART doesn't hound him. When he's ready, the clinic page shows the actual room and the actual audiologist's face and name. **(Anticipatory anxiety drops.)** At the visit, nobody rushes, nobody pressures, the price is shown plainly with a per-day framing, and the "no pressure" promise from the website is true in the room. **(Relief.)** Weeks later he sits at the head of the table and hears everyone. **(Belonging — the destination emotion.)** He tells a friend.

**Design must guarantee:** mirror-before-method, zero shaming, family-mediated entry, calm result, preserved autonomy, web-promise honored offline. Any element that makes Rajan feel old, rushed, or sold-to is a failure regardless of metrics.

## Narrative 2 — Priya, 38 (The Caring Daughter)

**Worry → overwhelm → relief at structure → confidence → empowered advocate.**

Priya manages her father's health and is terrified of two things: making the wrong choice and getting him oversold. She arrives via a blog article — "How to know if your elderly parent needs a hearing test." It's genuinely useful, sourced, reviewed by a named audiologist. **(Relief: this is a trustworthy source, not a sales funnel.)** It gives her conversation scripts for raising the topic without conflict. **(Overwhelm → structure.)**

She reads transparent pricing, an honest "screening is not a diagnosis," and a clear recommendation framework. **(Confidence: she can evaluate quality now.)** She uses "book for a family member," handles the logistics, and gets a confirmation that justifies the decision and reminds her it's reversible. **(Reassurance.)** She becomes the person who explains earKART to the rest of the family. **(Empowered advocate — destination.)**

**Design must guarantee:** depth and sourcing earn her trust; family-booking flow exists; transparency everywhere; scripts and frameworks, not just products.

## Narrative 3 — Arjun, 28 (The Young Sufferer)

**Denial → data-curiosity → private validation → discreet action → identity reframed.**

Arjun has tinnitus and tells himself he's "too young for this." He finds a noise-induced-hearing-loss article via search. It's data-driven and non-patronizing. **(Denial cracks — curiosity, on his terms.)** He takes the hearing check privately, fast, on his phone, with headphones. **(Private validation — no one has to know.)** The result is calm and points him to discreet, modern, nearly-invisible options and a "talk to an audiologist about tinnitus" path. **(The "hearing aid at 28" fear is reframed: this is tech, not decline.)** He books a discreet assessment and shares the hearing-protection score tool with friends. **(Identity reframed; becomes a low-friction advocate.)**

**Design must guarantee:** fast, discreet, data-rich, mobile-first; tinnitus-specific scent; invisibility addressed head-on; shareable social currency.

---

## Narrative 4 — Sunita, 34 (The Worried Parent)

**Panic → calm → clarity → trust → immediate, supported action.**

Sunita's pediatrician flagged possible hearing delay in her 4-year-old. She's searching at 1am in acute anxiety. The pediatric content is calm, authoritative, and de-escalating — it tells her what childhood hearing issues do and don't mean, in plain language, with absolute-risk framing. **(Panic → calm — the single most important transition for Sunita; the brand absorbs anxiety instead of amplifying it.)** It gives clear criteria and an immediate next step. **(Clarity.)** Named pediatric audiologists, real faces, "what to expect" with photos of a child-friendly room. **(Trust.)** She books quickly and is met with warmth, not alarm. **(Supported action.)**

**Design must guarantee:** maximum calm, zero fear-mongering on pediatric pages; immediate clear next step; specialist credibility; child-appropriate environment shown.

---

## Narrative 5 — Meera, 72 (The Informed Upgrader)

**Skepticism → respected intelligence → honest comparison → trust → smooth upgrade.**

Meera has 5-year-old aids and assumes she'll be upsold. She wants honest comparison and to be treated as the intelligent ex-teacher she is. The Solutions page organizes by her life, recommends based on fit (not margin), and separates independent information from earKART's recommendation. **(Skepticism softens — she feels respected, not handled.)** She reads honest outcome ranges and a named audiologist's profile she can request in advance. **(Trust.)** The upgrade is smooth, transparent, low-friction. **(Satisfaction → quiet loyalty.)**

**Design must guarantee:** anti-upsell integrity, fit-based recommendation, info/recommendation separation, respect for intelligence, continuity of named audiologist.

---

## Narrative 6 — Rohan, 45 (The Family Initiator)

**Concern → tactic-seeking → gentle pathway → confidence → gift of care.**

Rohan sees his mother's hearing declining and knows she'll resist. He needs a way in that won't trigger her defensiveness. He finds content framing a hearing check as an act of care, not a diagnosis, plus a "gift a hearing check" feature. **(From stuck to equipped.)** He's confident in the care quality from the depth and the real audiologist profiles. **(Confidence.)** He gifts the check; his mother (a Rajan-type) experiences Narrative 1. **(Care delivered without conflict.)**

**Design must guarantee:** "gift a hearing check" and family-booking flows; care-not-diagnosis framing; conversation support.

## Stakeholder Narratives (internal benchmarks)

**ENT / GP Referrer:** Skeptical of where they send patients → finds a clean "refer a patient" portal, visible clinical credentials, honest scope ("screening, not diagnosis"), and reliable follow-through → confident this won't embarrass them → becomes a recurring referral source. *Design must guarantee:* a credible, frictionless referral portal and clinical transparency.

**Audiologist (staff):** Wants the website to set accurate expectations so patients arrive informed and unafraid, not over-promised → pre-read patient context from the booking flow → patients arrive pre-framed by honest content → spends consultation on care, not myth-correction. *Design must guarantee:* honest pre-framing, no over-promising, context flowing to the clinician (systems thinking).

**Clinic Staff:** Needs bookings that arrive complete and correct, with realistic availability → clean CRM data, designed transitions, no over-promised callbacks → smooth front desk. *Design must guarantee:* data integrity, operational promises the team can keep.

**Customer Support:** Needs fewer "I got confused / where do I book / what did my result mean" tickets → clarity, pre-written errors, honest results, one voice → handles relationships, not avoidable confusion. *Design must guarantee:* comprehension, error scripting, channel-voice consistency.

**Investor:** Evaluates whether earKART is a durable, trust-led, defensible brand vs. a discount race → sees a patient-first, ethically rigorous, retention-oriented system with measurable funnels → confidence in long-term LTV and brand moat. *Design must guarantee:* the whole system reads as trust-led and measurable, not gimmick-led.

---

## How to use these narratives

1. **As an acceptance test:** for any new screen/flow, replay the relevant persona's arc through it. Does each emotional transition still happen in order? A screen that interrupts "defensive → seen" for Rajan, or "panic → calm" for Sunita, is rejected.
2. **As a tie-breaker:** when two designs both satisfy Phase A, pick the one that produces the cleaner emotional transition here.
3. **As a North Star for copy and imagery:** the specific moments (the dinner table, the 1am search, the gifted check) are reusable touchstones, not decoration.
