The basics

What hearing aids actually do.

A hearing aid is a precision device that amplifies and shapes sound to match your specific hearing profile — making speech clearer and everyday life easier. Modern devices do far more than make things louder: they reduce noise, manage feedback and adapt to different environments.

The right device depends on your hearing test, your lifestyle and your comfort — which is exactly why expert guidance matters.

Close-up of a discreet hearing aid
Expert guidance

How audiologists help you choose.

An audiologist turns test results and lifestyle into the right recommendation — transparently.

Read your hearing profile

Interpret your assessment across frequencies to match the right technology.

Balance needs & budget

Recommend options honestly — comfort and value, not just the top price.

Fit & fine-tune

Personalise settings and refine them over the first weeks of wear.

Features that matter

What to look for in a hearing aid.

  • Frequency channelsMore channels allow finer, more precise sound tuning.
  • Directional microphonesFocus on the voice in front of you in noisy places.
  • Noise reductionReduce background noise for clearer conversation.
  • Feedback managementPrevent whistling for comfortable all-day wear.
  • Bluetooth / wirelessStream calls and audio from your phone or TV.
  • Water resistanceEveryday protection against moisture and sweat.
  • Tinnitus maskersOptional soothing sound for tinnitus relief.
Styles

Types of hearing aids.

RIC

Receiver in Canal

A slim behind-the-ear body with the receiver placed inside the ear canal for natural, comfortable sound.

Suitable for
Mild to severe hearing loss

Discreet, comfortable and easy to service.

IIC

Invisible in Canal

The most discreet option, sitting deep in the canal so it is virtually invisible in everyday wear.

Suitable for
Mild to moderate hearing loss

Maximum discretion for those who prefer an invisible fit.

BTE

Behind the Ear

A robust device worn behind the ear, connected to a custom ear-mould — powerful and dependable.

Suitable for
Mild to profound hearing loss

Durable, easy to handle and suited to a wide range of needs.

ITE

In the Ear

A custom-moulded device that fills the outer ear, offering easy handling with reliable performance.

Suitable for
Mild to severe hearing loss

Comfortable custom fit with simple controls.

ITC

In the Canal

A compact custom device sitting partly in the canal — a balance of discretion and easy handling.

Suitable for
Mild to moderately-severe hearing loss

Subtle appearance with comfortable daily use.

CIC

Completely in Canal

A small custom device that sits fully within the canal for a discreet, low-profile look.

Suitable for
Mild to moderate hearing loss

Discreet fit with natural sound directionality.

Questions

Hearing aid FAQs

How do I know which hearing aid is right for me?

The right device depends on your hearing test results, lifestyle, comfort preferences and budget. An Earkart audiologist reviews all of these with you before recommending options — with no pressure to choose the most expensive model.

Are hearing aids difficult to use?

Modern hearing aids are designed for easy daily use, and many are rechargeable so there are no small batteries to change. We guide you through handling, cleaning and settings during fitting.

Do you offer support after I buy a hearing aid?

Yes. Fine-tuning over the first few weeks and ongoing after-sales support are an important part of the Earkart experience, including remote check-ins where suitable.

Can hearing aids help with tinnitus?

Some hearing aids include tinnitus-masking features. An audiologist can advise whether this may be suitable for you as part of a broader assessment.
